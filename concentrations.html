Concentrations (4.6 and 4.7)

The last two figures of page 4 show the result: the concentrations of the chosen compound (here 1,3-Di-o-tolylguanidine, batch 1) in all samples, and its stability in the QC samples over the whole run.

4.6 Concentration per class

A box per class (QCs, study groups, blanks) with the samples as points. The colour of a point tells whether its value was measured or replaced by the LOD/LLOQ rules:

Point Signal of the sample Value
dark grey: Measured at or above the mean signal of the LLOQ level the calculated concentration
red: Not detected below the LOD signal LLOQ / 4
orange: Detected from the LOD signal to below the LLOQ level LLOQ / 2
blue: Set to LLOQ at or above the LLOQ level, but calculated below the LLOQ the LLOQ

With the processing option Replace values below the LOD/LLOQ unticked, the red and orange values have no number: they are reported as n.d. (not detected) and n.q. (not quantified), left out of the plots and statistics, and their number is given under the title. Use the Linear / Log buttons for the scale.

How to read it

  • Differences between the study groups are what you are after; the QCs should be tight.
  • Many replaced values in a group mean the compound is mostly below the LLOQ there; its average is then driven by the replacement values, so look at the measured values only (the exported statistics and report 4.6.1 use measured values only).

4.7 QC samples in acquisition order

The compound in the QC samples of all batches along the injection order (each batch quantified with its own calibration), a line per QC group. Open points are values replaced by the LOD/LLOQ rules.

How to read it

  • A flat line per QC group means the whole procedure (normalization, blank subtraction, calibration) gives the same concentration from the start to the end of the run.
  • A step between batches points to a batch effect in the calibration (e.g. a calibration curve of one batch that differs from the others); compare the batches’ curves in 4.4.
  • A drift within a batch that normalization did not remove points to a compound that its internal standard does not follow well (see 3.8).
