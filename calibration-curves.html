Calibration curves per chemical (4.4)

For every targeted compound (Quant in the MS-DIAL comment), QuaNTA fits a calibration curve to the calibration samples (Cal… classes) and uses it to turn the signal of every sample into a concentration. Figure 4.4 of the app shows the curve of the compound selected in table 4.2. Below is the same figure as in the app, with the example data (batch 1, Multivariate (PCA) normalization); here, choose the compound in the list or search it by name.

The figure has two modes; switch with the buttons, or with the arrow keys ← and →. The arrow keys ↑ and ↓ go to the previous and next compound (in the app, the rows of table 4.2).

  • Calibration inspection (first): every calibration point, each injection of a calibration sample, at its spiked (nominal) concentration and its measured signal. Points of the same concentration are replicates; with several batches, the symbol tells the batch. The colours tell the role of each level:
    • Cal0 (red): the zero calibrant. Its signal gives the thresholds of the LOD (3×) and the LLOQ (10×, or the mean plus 3 / 10 standard deviations). On log axes, a concentration of 0 has no place: Cal0 is a short line at the left edge, at its signal.
    • LOD (blue): the lowest level whose mean signal is above the LOD threshold.
    • Within limits (green): the levels the curve is fitted on, from the LLOQ upwards.
    • Above the ULOQ (olive): levels in the curve but above its linear range.
    • Out-of-range (grey): levels not used for the curve.
  • Final calibration model: the fitted curve, the calibration points it was fitted on (black), and the QCs (blue; the QC group in the hover text) and study samples (by class) at the concentration the curve gives them. Values replaced by the LOD/LLOQ rules are open circles; their hover text tells the rule and the calculated concentration.

In both modes:

  • Shading: dark grey below the LOD, light grey below the LLOQ, and olive above the ULOQ (dashed line), the top of the linear range. Labels at the top name each limit in its colour: LOD (blue) and LLOQ (purple), with a dotted line, or LOD = LLOQ (orange) when they are the same level, and ULOQ (olive). The ULOQ is always the actual top of the linear range: when the curve keeps more levels (the minimum number of calibration levels), those levels are in the olive area.
  • Above the plot, the parameters of the curve (model, R², LOD, LLOQ, ULOQ, highest calibration concentration, the curve used) and notes, e.g. why a curve is not valid.
  • The title is coloured as the row of the compound in table 4.2: red when it has no valid curve, orange when it was kept although not valid, blue when it uses the global curve, grey when it was removed before calibration.
  • Curve (only for a compound that uses the global curve, with several batches): the calibration points of all batches, which the global curve was fitted on, or only those of the batch.

How to read it

  • Replicates should lie close together. A level with widely spread replicates is a sign of a pipetting or injection problem; with the RSD% option, the LLOQ level must have an RSD% of at most 20%.
  • Samples in the white area are within the calibration range and quantified.
  • Samples in the grey areas are below the LLOQ (light) or the LOD (dark). Depending on the setting Replace values below the LOD/LLOQ, their concentrations are replaced (LLOQ/2 or LLOQ/4, open circles) or reported as n.q. / n.d..
  • Samples in the olive area are above the ULOQ: within the curve but above its linear range, or extrapolated above its highest level. Treat these values with care, or dilute and re-measure.
  • A curve far from its calibration points, or points bending away at the top, suggests that the linear range is shorter than the calibration range; see the notes in table 4.2 and the option Enforce linear range.
TipIn the app

Use the arrow keys ↑ / ↓ to step through the rows of table 4.2 and ← / → to switch the mode; the figure follows at once. With the processing option Log scale for calibration curve fitting, the figure starts with log axes, which show the low concentrations better.
