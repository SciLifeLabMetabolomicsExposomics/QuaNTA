Calibration curves (4.4 and 4.5)

For every targeted compound (Quant in the MS-DIAL comment), QuaNTA fits a calibration curve to the calibration samples (Cal… classes) and uses it to turn the signal of every sample into a concentration. Section 4 of the app shows the curves one compound at a time: choose the compound in table 4.2, and figures 4.4 and 4.5 follow.

The examples below show LysoPC(18:1) in batch 1.

4.4 The calibration points

Each point is one injection of a calibration sample: its spiked (nominal) concentration on the x axis and its measured signal on the y axis. Points of the same concentration are replicates; with several batches, the symbol tells the batch.

How to read it

  • Colours tell the role of each level:
    • Cal0 (red): the zero calibrant. Its signal gives the thresholds of the LOD (3×) and the LLOQ (10×, or the mean plus 3 / 10 standard deviations).
    • LOD (blue): the lowest level whose mean signal is above the LOD threshold.
    • Within limits (green): the levels the curve is fitted on, from the LLOQ upwards.
    • Above the ULOQ (olive): levels in the curve but above its linear range.
    • Out-of-range (grey): levels not used for the curve.
  • Shading: dark grey below the LOD, light grey below the LLOQ, and olive above the ULOQ (dashed line), the top of the linear range.
  • Replicates should lie close together. A level with widely spread replicates is a sign of a pipetting or injection problem; with the RSD% option, the LLOQ level must have an RSD% of at most 20%.

4.5 The samples on the curve

The line is the fitted calibration curve (linear-1/x2 for this compound). The black points are the calibration samples, the coloured ones the QCs and the study samples by class, placed at the concentration the curve gives them.

How to read it

  • Samples in the white area are within the calibration range and quantified.
  • Samples in the grey areas are below the LLOQ (light) or the LOD (dark). Depending on the setting Replace values below the LOD/LLOQ, their concentrations are replaced (LLOQ/2 or LLOQ/4) or reported as n.q. / n.d..
  • Samples in the olive area are above the ULOQ: within the curve but above its linear range, or extrapolated above its highest level. Treat these values with care, or dilute and re-measure.
  • A curve far from its calibration points, or points bending away at the top, suggests that the linear range is shorter than the calibration range; see the notes in table 4.2 and the option Enforce linear range.
TipIn the app

Use the arrow keys ↑ / ↓ in table 4.2 to step through the compounds; both figures follow at once. With the processing option Log scale for calibration curve fitting, both figures have log axes, which show the low concentrations better.
