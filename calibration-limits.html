Calibration limits (4.1–4.3)

Page 4 of the app, Calibration and quantification, turns the signals of the targeted compounds into concentrations. At the top you choose the normalization method (the best one, Multivariate (PCA), is preselected) and the batch; the tables and figures follow. The examples below show batch 1.

How a calibration curve is made, in short:

  1. The zero calibrant (Cal0) gives two signal thresholds: 3× and 10× its mean signal (or its mean plus 3 and 10 standard deviations).
  2. The LOD is the lowest calibration level whose mean signal is above the first threshold, the LLOQ the lowest above the second (and, with the RSD% option, with an RSD% of at most 20%).
  3. The curve is fitted from the LLOQ upwards: linear or quadratic, weighted 1, 1/x or 1/x², the combination with the highest R².
  4. The ULOQ is the top of the curve’s linear range (see 4.2 and the next chapter).

4.1 Calibration curve samples

The number of calibration samples per concentration level in the chosen batch: the replicates the curve of this batch is fitted on.

4.2 Calibration curve limits

One row per targeted compound with its limits in the chosen batch. In the app, select a row (or step with the arrow keys ↑ / ↓) to show that compound in figures 4.4–4.7.

How to read it

  • Calibration: the curve used for the compound in this batch: the batch’s own (Batch), the curve of all batches together (Global, blue rows), or none (red rows).

  • LOD and LLOQ are calibration levels; values below them are replaced or reported as n.d. / n.q. (see 4.6).

  • Maximum conc. is the highest level the curve is fitted on; the ULOQ is the top of its linear range. They differ when the top levels bend away from the line and the curve was not cut there (option Enforce linear range). A quadratic curve has no ULOQ.

  • Notes show an icon per kind of note; hover an icon for the full text:

    • No valid curve: No valid curve: e.g. too few levels above the LLOQ, no signal, or R² below the minimum. The row is red.
    • Kept although not valid: Kept although not valid (option Keep all calibration curves). The row is orange.
    • Global curve used: The batch’s own curve is not valid, or the batch has too few replicates: the global curve is used.
    • Linear range (ULOQ): Linear range: levels above the ULOQ, which were cut off or are above the linear range.
    • Detection in samples: Detection in the samples: not detected in any study sample, or detected but below the LLOQ (not exported).
    • Removed before calibration: Removed before calibration by the blank filter or the QC RSD% filter. The row is grey.

The exported tables have the same notes in full, as text.

4.3 Chemicals with concentrations

The number of compounds per batch with a valid calibration curve and concentrations in the samples. Compounds without any study sample at or above the LLOQ are not quantified and not exported.
